Ground Truth, order count in the morning window
09:15-09:35  20 orders, a Thursday

Counted, not estimated: one mark per completed order for twenty minutes.

The calendar date is not recorded, only the weekday, which is what the model
takes: params/schedule/thursday.yaml is the day this is compared against and
the day the hybrid was fitted on. Thursday predicts 20.1 and twenty were
counted.

Why this window was chosen, and what the count decides.

Nobody counted anything before 10:20 on 2026-09-03, so the free-rate fit in
params/fitted_arrivals.yaml has no data for the morning at all: its early bins
still hold the flat 20/hour that sim/fit_profile.py seeds its search with. The
room schedule says 520 students leave classes in this building at 09:15. One of
those is a placeholder and one is a measurement, and until now nothing had told
them apart.

Predicted orders in this window, 20 simulated days each:

    fitted profile      7.3   [ 2-12]
    hybrid             20.1   [10-26]
    timetable          16.8   [12-20]

Twenty is the hybrid's mean and did not occur once in twenty days of the free
fit. That excludes it rather than merely disfavouring it, and it is the first
number in this project that was predicted before it was counted -- the schedule
route was built from a registrar's export and fitted only against the *midday*
queue, so the morning was never fitted to anything.

The weekday changes the prediction but not the conclusion. Applying the same
three fitted parameters to each day's own schedule:

    monday      15.7  [ 8-21]
    tuesday     16.5  [ 8-26]
    wednesday   17.4  [ 9-29]
    thursday    20.1  [10-26]
    friday      13.7  [ 6-19]

A count of twenty is consistent with any weekday except Friday, and excludes
the free fit on every one of them. It landed on Thursday, which is both the
closest prediction and the day the model was fitted against, so the comparison
is like for like.

It also settles an apparent contradiction in the first day of observation,
which recorded "not a lot of people around 9:00 to 10:40". Twenty orders in
twenty minutes is sixty an hour, which sounds busy. Both are true: the model
has a queue of 3.5 people in this window against a rate of one order a minute.
A short line and a steady rate is a fast cafe, not a quiet one -- and it is
exactly the distinction the queue counts alone could never draw, because they
measure the line and not the throughput.

Still missing, and now the cheapest thing left: the queue depth in this same
window. Rate is counted and the line is not, so Little's Law cannot be closed
here yet. Count the line every couple of minutes while tallying orders and
W = L / lambda gives an observed wait with no model in it.

Consequence for what customers are told: the app quotes 6.8 minutes at 09:00
from the hybrid, against 2.8 from the free fit. The count says the longer quote
is the honest one.

Shelf tally, same visit: 8 orders, 2 with food, both of those with a drink too.
Six were a single drink. So 10 items, 20% of them food, mean basket 1.25.

Eight orders cannot pin a share -- the 95% interval on 2 of 8 runs from 7% to
59%, which at roughly $3 of daily margin per point is worth +/-$156 on a figure
of about $2000. What it does settle is structure rather than level: food came
with a drink both times, and the model was generating a quarter of every day's
orders as a sandwich with no coffee.
